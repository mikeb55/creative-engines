# COMPOSER OS V3.4 — ADVANCED ARRANGING, ORCHESTRATION & FILM SCORING
## Research and Implementation Specification

Prepared 17 October 2026 · Repository: [`mikeb55/creative-engines`](https://github.com/mikeb55/creative-engines) · Audited commit: `378b90d0` ("label the DEV desktop build Composer OS DEV — V3.3") · V3.3 baseline smoke: 819 passed, 144 suites, 5 known failures, 0 unexpected ([V3.3 Release Report](https://github.com/mikeb55/creative-engines/blob/378b90d0/docs/V3.3-RELEASE-REPORT.md))

## 1. Verdict summary

**BUILD V3.4**, within the five-stage scope below.

The architecture already has the right layers:

- orchestration plans with role, register, texture and density ownership;
- quartet and big-band planners;
- about 20 string-quartet harmonic-voice (VRB) profiles, including Bartók, Ligeti, Lachenmann, Crumb and Reich/Glass;
- motif operators;
- independence and coherence evidence.

The real constraint is the **score model and MusicXML exporter**, which cannot yet express what orchestration needs:

- only three articulations exist;
- there are no slurs or hairpins;
- dynamics are written only on the first part;
- there are no string techniques, mutes or divisi;
- transposition is chromatic only;
- there is no instrument catalogue beyond guitar, bass, quartet and four big-band section parts;
- there is no tempo map or hit-point model.

V3.4 therefore starts with notation and instrument data, adds evidence before generation, and only then builds an opt-in **Orchestrate** transformer that reuses existing engines.

Scope is already reduced:

- video playback, sample-library mapping, audio rendering, graphic notation, aleatoric box notation in export, and from-scratch symphonic composition are **DEFER**;
- quality scores and auto-correction are **REJECT**.

## 3. Research findings by domain

Throughout this section, a **documented** technique is taken from a cited treatise or specification. **Inference** marks an engineering heuristic derived from those sources, which must be listened to before it is trusted.

### 3.1 String quartet and chamber music

- **Ranges and techniques (documented).**
  - Violin G3–A7, harmonic to D8 ([VSL Violin](https://www.vsl.co.at/academy/strings/violin)).
  - Viola C3–A6, alto clef, treble from the second octave above middle C ([VSL Viola](https://www.vsl.co.at/academy/strings/viola)).
  - Cello C2–A5 ([VSL Cello](https://www.vsl.co.at/academy/strings/cello)).
  - Double stops use two adjacent strings. "More than two strings cannot be sounded at the same time", so triple and quadruple stops are arpeggiated. Natural harmonics are generally used up to the fifth partial. The common artificial harmonic sounds two octaves above the stopped note ([VSL Violin](https://www.vsl.co.at/academy/strings/violin)).
- **Melodic relief (documented).** "Melody should always stand out in relief from the accompaniment… by selection and contrast of timbres, strengthening of resonance by doubling… or crossing of parts." Prolonged doubling "should be avoided; it is only permissible in isolated phrases" ([Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm), ch. II and V).
- **Orchestration and identity (documented).** "Orchestration is part of the very soul of the work" ([Rimsky-Korsakov, Preface](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm)). For the software, this means instrumentation must be planned with the material, not applied afterwards as distribution.
- **Independence (documented).** V3.3 already uses Huron's perceptual principles and Fux in `independenceReport.ts`.
- **Notation (documented).** MusicXML supports `<up-bow>`, `<down-bow>`, `<harmonic>`, `<open-string>`, `<snap-pizzicato>` and `<fingering>` inside `<technical>` ([MusicXML technical](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/technical/)). `<sound pizzicato="yes|no">` switches playback between pizz. and arco for all following notes ([MusicXML sound](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/sound/)). Unmeasured tremolo is a tremolo type ([musicxml.xsd](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/listings/musicxml.xsd/)).

### 3.2 Big band and jazz orchestration

- **The repository already holds the research.** `big-band/data/BigBandResearch.md` covers Ellington, Basie, Thad Jones, Schneider, Gil Evans, Brookmeyer and Oliver Nelson, plus eras, swing, blues and harmony, with ten engine rules each for shout choruses, riffs and soli. V3.4 consumes these rules; it does not re-research them.
- **Section voicing (documented).** Four-way close ("block") voicing in an AATTB saxophone section is described in Nestico's *The Complete Arranger* ([excerpt](https://www.scribd.com/document/448972333/352470533-The-Complete-Arranger-Sammy-Nestico-pdf)). Drop-2 and open voicings are standard section techniques ([JazzLib overview](https://jazzlib.com/es/blog/chord-voicing-techniques-big-band-arranging)).
- **Transpositions (documented).**
  - Alto sounds a major sixth lower, tenor a major ninth lower, and baritone an octave plus a sixth lower than written ([Orchestralibrary ranges](https://www.orchestralibrary.com/reftables/rang.html)).
  - The B♭ trumpet sounds a major second lower ([VSL Trumpet in C](https://www.vsl.co.at/academy/brass/trumpet-c)).
  - Written ranges: horn F♯2–C6, B♭ trumpet F♯3–D6, saxophones B♭3–G6. Newer baritones extend to written A, which is concert C2 ([Orchestralibrary](https://www.orchestralibrary.com/reftables/rang.html)).
  - Tenor trombone E2–F5, non-transposing ([VSL Tenor trombone](https://www.vsl.co.at/academy/brass/tenor-trombone)).
- **Gap.** True soli and shout writing need individual section parts. The current score has one part per section (see 2.2).

### 3.3 Symphonic orchestration

- **Balance (documented).** In loud passages, "1 Trumpet = 1 Trombone = 1 Tuba = 2 Horns" and "1 Horn = 2 Clarinets = 2 Oboes = 2 Flutes = 2 Bassoons". In piano passages "all wind-instruments, wood or brass are of fairly equal balance" ([Rimsky-Korsakov, ch. I](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm)). This is a published weighting, so the balance evidence can use it without inventing one.

- **Texture and timbre (documented, perceptual research).** McAdams, Goodchild and Soden's taxonomy of orchestral grouping effects has three groups ([MTO 28.3](https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.22.28.3/mto.22.28.3.mcadams.html)):
  - *concurrent*: blend, timbral augmentation, timbral emergence, non-blend, timbral heterogeneity;
  - *sequential*: stream integration, surface texture, stream segregation, stratification;
  - *segmental*: timbral contrasts, shifts, echoes, antiphonal contrasts, juxtapositions, sectional boundaries.

  These labels give the evidence report a vocabulary grounded in research.
- **ORCHARD.** The associated database holds 4,439 annotations over 66 movements, but "because of copyright issues" it is available "only to research partners" ([ORCHARD](https://orchard.actor-project.org/)). **It must not be used as data.** This is a licensing finding.
- **Treatises.**
  - Rimsky-Korsakov's *Principles* is in the public domain on [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/33900).
  - The Berlioz–Strauss *Treatise* (Front translation, Kalmus 1948) is on [IMSLP](http://imslp.info/files/imglnks/usimg/3/30/IMSLP11615-Treatise_on_InstrumentationBerlioz.pdf), with the [Dover edition on archive.org](https://archive.org/details/treatiseoninstru0000berl). Copyright of the translation varies by country, so cite it but do not copy text.
  - Adler's *The Study of Orchestration*, 4th edition ([Norton](https://wwnorton.com/books/9780393920659)), and Blatter's *Instrumentation and Orchestration*, 2nd edition ([archive.org record](https://archive.org/details/instrumentationo0000blat_u5w6)), are copyrighted. Use them as reference for human verification only; do not encode their tables verbatim.

### 3.4 Film scoring

- **Timecode (documented).** Timecodes use the format hh:mm:ss:ff "and can be used as a reference tool" ([Dorico timecodes](https://archive.steinberg.help/dorico/v2/en/dorico/topics/notation_reference/notation_reference_timecodes_c.html)). Dorico Pro can "find appropriate tempos based on where important markers occur" ([Dorico video](https://archive.steinberg.help/dorico/v3/en/dorico/topics/setup_mode/setup_mode_video_c.html)). Tempo fitting to hit points is therefore established professional practice.
- **Click-track arithmetic (documented).** A beat lasts 60/BPM seconds ([BPM formulas](https://zencalculators.com/music/bpm)). Frames per beat = fps × 60 / BPM, which is the classic frame-click basis ([Lacey, frame clicks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5vguipjQiYc)).
- **Process.** The *Complete Guide to Film Scoring* covers cue preparation, timing and recording ([Berklee Library guide](https://guides.library.berklee.edu/filmscoring)).
- **MusicXML support.** Tempo changes use `<metronome>` and `<sound tempo>`, metric modulation uses `<metronome-relation>` ([MusicXML metronome](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/metronome/)), and `<offset>` places directions within a bar ([MusicXML offset](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/offset/)).
- **Inference.** Thematic transformation per segment can reuse the V3.3 motif chains and BHL worlds. Dramatic intensity can drive the existing density and register planners.

### 3.5 Experimental and contemporary

- **Spectral (documented).** Grisey's *Partiels* opening derives from a sonogram analysis of a trombone low E2, with the components re-created by instruments ([Wikipedia: Partiels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partiels)). Grisey called this "instrumental synthesis" ([SMCQ](https://smcq.qc.ca/smcq/en/oeuvre/22433-partiels)).
- **Limited aleatory (documented).** Lutosławski "introduced limited aleatoric elements, while retaining tight control" of material and architecture ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witold_Lutos%C5%82awski)). The technique is *ad libitum* playing at marked points ([Lutosławski Society](https://www.lutoslawski.org.pl/en/witold-lutoslawski/style-and-though)). MusicXML has no native ad-lib box, so export would be text plus repeat graphics only (**DEFER**).
- **Process music (documented).** Reich: "I am interested in perceptible processes" ([Wikipedia: Steve Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Reich)); *Piano Phase* applies phasing to live performers ([stevereich.com](https://stevereich.com/composition/piano-phase/)). This is EXISTING as the `reich_glass` SQ VRB (89 `RG_*` receipts, per `CLAUDE.md`).
- **Extended techniques (documented).** Lachenmann's *musique concrète instrumentale* uses extended techniques that often produce noise-like timbres ([Routledge REM](https://www.rem.routledge.com/articles/lachenmann-helmut-friedrich-1935-1)). This is EXISTING as the `lachenmann` VRB. The notation gap is in 2.4.

- Any orchestration or "film quality" score.
- Automatic correction of balance, ranges or parallels. These are reported, never silently fixed; out-of-range notes are flagged, not moved, except by the explicit whole-phrase octave rule.
- New "sounds like [living film composer]" templates.
- Rewriting the String Quartet, Big Band or ECM generators onto N2.
- Using ORCHARD, Adler or Blatter tables as data.
- Editing WWH, STABLE or DEV installations.

## 6. Algorithms and constraints

### 6.1 Melody preservation (N2, invariant)

1. Detect the melody layer. Use the existing role tags if the source came from Composer OS. Otherwise take the highest-salience line: highest sustained part, with ties broken by the hook and motif matches from V3.3.
2. Choose a carrier from `melodyCarriers` whose comfort range covers the phrase. If none does, move the **whole phrase** by octaves (±12k) to the nearest fit. Never move individual notes.
3. Pitch-class sequence, rhythm and interval sequence must be identical to the source. The receipt records `octaveShift`. A test proves the interval vector is preserved.
4. If no carrier fits even after octave moves, the phrase stays in the best carrier and the notes are flagged `outside_hard_range`.

### 6.2 Doubling (documented, Rimsky ch. V)

- Doubling in unison or octave is allowed per section, but a run cannot exceed `maxDoublingBars` (default 4).
- After the cap, the doubling instrument rests or switches to the counterline.
- Reported in `doublingRuns`.

### 6.3 Derived independent lines

- A counterline is a V3.3 development chain applied to a source motif, for example invert-in-field then augment ×2, placed at a rhythmic offset (default one beat). This uses existing `motif-ops`.
- The transformer accepts a derived line only if, against the melody, the independence report shows:
  - co-onset rate ≤ 0.5;
  - no parallel perfect run longer than 2.

  These thresholds are **inference** and configurable. They are acceptance filters for candidate selection, not quality claims. If no candidate passes, the line is omitted and the receipt says so.

### 6.4 Harmony fill

Inner voices come from one of:

- the source harmony;
- a BHL world (`bhl/bhlWorlds.ts`);
- Pentatonic or Hexatonic colour;
- an SQ harmonic voice.

They are voiced by the existing `voicing` planners and checked by `quartetVoiceLeadingGuard` logic for strings. For big-band full, X5 writes four-way close or drop-2 beneath the lead, using documented section practice.

### 6.5 Density and register over time

`SectionColourPlan.density` and `registerCentre` feed `density/densityCurvePlanner.ts` and `register-map/registerMapPlanner.ts`. Activity per part is controlled through the existing `silenceEligibleByBar`.

### 6.6 Spacing

These rules are **inference** from common practice, flagged rather than enforced:

- spacing wider than an octave between adjacent upper voices;
- close intervals (≤ 4 semitones) below about E3 (MIDI 52).

The thresholds are configurable and appear in `spacing` only.

### 6.7 Balance (documented weights)

- **Loud bands:** trumpet = trombone = tuba = 2; horn = 1; woodwind = 0.5. Saxophone = 1 (**inference**, not in Rimsky).
- **Soft bands:** all winds = 1.
- **Strings:** evidence only, as `strings_section` = 1 per desk (**inference**).
- Melody and accompaniment weights are reported per bar. There is no pass/fail.

### 6.8 Tempo-map solver (N4)

1. Convert SMPTE to seconds:
   - frames ÷ fps for 24, 25 and 30;
   - for 23.976 non-drop, frames × 1001 / 24000;
   - for 29.97 drop-frame, use standard drop-frame counting.
2. Constant-tempo pass: find the BPM in range that puts each `must` hit on its target (downbeat or beat), minimising maximum error. Accept if every `must` error ≤ `toleranceFrames`.
3. Otherwise, split at the worst hit and allow up to `maxChanges` tempo events. Solve each segment independently (piecewise constant) and keep integer BPM if requested.
4. Report the frame error per hit, and list any unmet hits. The solver never shifts a hit.

### 6.9 Spectral chord (N5)

- Partial n of fundamental f₀ has frequency n·f₀ and pitch 69 + 12·log₂(n·f₀/440).
- Round to semitone or quarter-tone. Quarter-tones are exported with `<alter>` ±0.5 for notation only; playback is deferred.
- Assign partials to instruments whose comfort range covers them, lowest partials to the lowest instruments. Dynamics decrease with partial number (**inference** from instrumental-synthesis practice).

### 6.10 Determinism and protection

- Every module is pure, seeded and deterministic.
- New features run only through new app-API entries (`app-api/orchestrateApp.ts`, `filmApp.ts`) or explicit flags.
- No existing preset calls N2, N4 or N5.
- X1–X3 export changes run only when the new fields are present, and golden tests prove byte-identical output otherwise.

## 11. Human listening and score-review requirements

Mike's review is the only route to approval. Each item is recorded in `musical-evaluation/humanReviewRecord.ts`, and `musicallyApproved` stays `false` until then.

### Reference outputs (generated in Stage E, fixed seeds)

| # | Output | Review |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Duo (WWH-style source, not a locked WWH score) → string quartet | Melody recognisable in Vn I? Counterline independent by ear? Cello register sensible? |
| H2 | Same duo → Pierrot (fl, cl, vn, vc, pno) | Blend or heterogeneity heard as the report states? |
| H3 | Riff → full big band (sectionSplit) shout | Sax soli lead audible? Brass voicings clear, not muddy below E3? |
| H4 | Quartet → chamber orchestra, A/B sections | Sectional boundary audible at the bar the report names? |
| H5 | 60-second film cue, 3 hits | Hits land within 1 frame against a test video at 24 fps |
| H6 | Spectral chord on E2 | Fuses into a single colour, or splits? |

### Procedure

1. **Notation import.** Open each MusicXML in Sibelius, toggling Concert and Transposed pitch. Confirm:
   - transposed parts read correctly, especially T1–T6 instruments;
   - clefs are right (viola alto, cello tenor where high, horn treble/bass);
   - slurs, hairpins, pizz./arco and mutes appear;
   - bar maths has no errors.
2. **Playback listening.** Use Sibelius playback, or your DAW through the existing Composer OS player, for H1–H6. Listen twice:
   - once at full score;
   - once soloing the melody carrier with and without accompaniment, to check melodic relief.
3. **Balance check.** For two loud and two soft bars per output, write whether the melody is audible. Compare with `balanceByBar`. Disagreements are logged as evidence-model feedback, not as score changes.
4. **Playability.** For H1, H3 and H4, ask one string player and one brass or sax player to read their parts. Record:
   - impossible double stops;
   - awkward slurs or bowings;
   - endurance problems in high brass;
   - breathing problems in long sax lines.
5. **Film timing.** Import the H5 MusicXML and click into the DAW at the cue's fps. Place the hit markers and measure the frame error at each hit. It passes if every `must` hit is ≤ 1 frame.
6. **Artistic decision.** Record a keep, revise or discard decision per output, with one sentence of reasoning. No output becomes an approved preset without this record.
