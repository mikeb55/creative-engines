# Film Composer Harmonic Voices — User Guide

When you work in **String Quartet → SQ→BB** (sketch a quartet, then translate to big band), the **SQ Harmonic Voice** picker tells Composer OS which composer's habits should shape the four string lines before the arrangement step. Each choice is a musical profile, not a sound library: it steers phrase length, harmonic colour, how much the voices move together, and how the sketch pairs with a big-band arranging style on the other side of the workflow.

This guide describes what you should *hear* when you pick each name. None of these replace your harmony input or form choices—they colour the contrapuntal behaviour of the generated quartet texture.

---

## Philip Glass

**In one sentence:** A tight, hypnotic mid-range weave where short patterns repeat and slowly thicken, staying in one modal world without classical cadences.

**Phrase shape:** Phrases are built from **very short loops** (roughly two to four bars) that repeat with little sense of “ending.” Harmonic changes can mark a new loop, but you will not get a tidy V–I finish. The music is designed to feel continuous—wall-to-wall texture is correct, not a mistake.

**Harmonic colour:** Simple triads and modal centres (Aeolian, Dorian, Phrygian flavours), often with added sixths and ninths. Harmony stays **stable and non-functional**—one tonal area, no dramatic modulations.

**Register:** Voices stay in a **comfortable middle band**; lines expand outward gradually rather than leaping from bass rumble to screaming high violin. The overall spread is **narrow** compared with epic film scores.

**Best paired with:** **Schneider** (primary) or **Brookmeyer** (secondary)—colouristic, chamber-jazz orchestration that respects sustained patterns and does not force swing-era shout-chorus clichés onto minimal material.

**Sounds like:** *Mishima*, *The Hours*, *Notes on a Scandal* (Glass's own film scores—not the Reich-style process-music profile elsewhere in the app).

**Not this if you want:** Clear cadences, wide heroic brass arcs, or thriller-style extreme registers.

## Nino Rota

**In one sentence:** Singable, old-world melodies in the soprano with polite periodic phrasing and warm triad-based harmony—like a Neapolitan tune dressed for the cinema.

**Phrase shape:** **Short, regular phrases** (often four to eight bars) with a strong sense of **tonal cadence**—phrases feel like they “arrive” even when the harmony is bittersweet. Inner voices may double the tune in thirds and sixths; the top line leads.

**Harmonic colour:** Triads and mild dominants, **chromatic mediant colour** (unexpected key shifts by third relationship), and occasional added-note warmth—not harsh modernism. Harmony behaves **functionally** more than modally.

**Register:** The melody sits in a **lyrical soprano arch**; accompaniment stays relatively centred—not whisper-quiet, not sky-high. Spread is **moderate and centrist**.

**Best paired with:** **Schneider** (primary) or **Brookmeyer** (secondary)—lyrical orchestration that can honour a singing top line without turning everything into a Basie shout chart.

**Sounds like:** *The Godfather*, *Amarcord*, *8½*, *La Dolce Vita*.

**Not this if you want:** Ostinato-driven minimalism, atonal clusters, or endless loops without cadence.

---

## Maurice Ravel

**In one sentence:** Luminous, refined quartet writing with motivic echoes, rich extended chords, and classical phrase rhythm—impressionist harmony in a chamber dress.

**Phrase shape:** **Periodic, cadential phrases**—often two-to-four-bar cells gathered into longer periods with a sense of punctuation. Motifs return and vary (echo and inversion), so the texture feels composed rather than improvised.

**Harmonic colour:** **Extended tertian harmony** (sevenths through thirteenths), half-diminished colour, and **octatonic tinges**—shimmering stacks rather than folk triads alone. Parallel harmonic colour shifts are part of the vocabulary.

**Register:** Comfortable chamber range with **occasional climactic leaps** in the top line—not Herrmann-wide, but more colour than Rota's strict centrisim. Spread is **moderate**, controlled.

**Best paired with:** **Schneider** (primary) or **Brookmeyer** (secondary)—orchestral colour and contrapuntal voicing that suit impressionist harmony without flattening it into swing clichés.

**Sounds like:** Ravel's *String Quartet in F*, and the harmonic world of *Daphnis*-era orchestral writing adapted to four parts.

**Not this if you want:** Minimal looping cells, thriller ostinatos, or blockbuster brass fanfares.

## Angelo Badalamenti

**In one sentence:** A single long emotional arc—dark opening, gradual climb, brief peak, then a dissolving coda—over a sustained bass pedal and smoky jazz extensions in the middle voices.

**Phrase shape:** Think **one through-composed phrase of sixteen to twenty bars**, not a chain of pop hooks. Inside that arch: a **dark intro**, a **long climb**, a **short climax**, then a **coda that melts away** (returning toward the opening mood counts as a real ending, not a failure to cadence). There is **no classical perfect cadence**; harmonic motion circles through **chromatic mediants** (C→E→F→A♭ type motion) instead of fifth-based tonality.

**Harmonic colour:** **Aeolian / natural minor** field with **cool-jazz chords**—minor sevenths, major ninths, add-nine colour, Lydian sharp-eleventh tints. Inner voices hold **beautiful dissonances** (sevenths and extensions) over a **fixed pedal**; the bass may stay on one root while harmony shifts above it.

**Register:** **Mid-register compression**: melody on top, chromatic cushion in the inner strings, pedal in the bass. The band stays **narrow-to-moderate** in total pitch height—intimate, not symphonic.

**Best paired with:** **Schneider** (primary) or **Brookmeyer** (secondary)—lush but restrained orchestration that preserves space and modal mystery; avoid aggressive swing-band economy that fights the pedal and extensions.

**Sounds like:** *Twin Peaks* (“Laura Palmer's Theme”), *Blue Velvet*, *Mulholland Drive*, *The Straight Story*.

**Not this if you want:** Short repeating ostinatos (Glass/Zimmer territory), strict four-bar song form (Rota), or functional V–I Hollywood cadences (Williams).

## Ennio Morricone

**In one sentence:** A folk-tinged, modal top-line melody over long pedals and suspended harmony—lyrical, spacious, and willing to end inconclusively.

**Phrase shape:** **Long arches** (eight bars and beyond) that often **suspend** rather than cadence strongly. Endings may feel **open or modal** (♭VI–♭VII–i colour) rather than a bright perfect authentic close. **Pedal points** can hold for many bars without the engine “forcing” a change.

**Harmonic colour:** **Modal mixture**—Aeolian, Dorian, Phrygian, Mixolydian—and **spaghetti-western cadence colours** (chromatic mediants, ♭III–V half-cadence flavour). Triads with add-six/add-nine sheen; drones and dominant/tonic pedals.

**Register:** A **lyrical arch** in the melody with accompaniment that supports rather than competes; spread is **moderate**, poetic rather than gigantic.

**Best paired with:** **Schneider** (primary) or **Mingus** (secondary)—Schneider for cinematic colour; Mingus when you want more grit and blues inflection under the same modal melody.

**Sounds like:** *The Mission*, *Once Upon a Time in the West*, *Cinema Paradiso*, *The Good, the Bad and the Ugly* (melodic side).

**Not this if you want:** Tight minimal loops, strict periodic tonality, or extreme high/low thriller registers.

## Jerry Goldsmith

**In one sentence:** Volatile, cinematic lines that leap across a wide range with chromatic jazz harmony and phrases that develop in open-ended cells rather than neat eight-bar tunes.

**Phrase shape:** **Doubly open cells**—material grows and shifts in **short bursts** (roughly four to six bars) without insisting on a final cadence every time. Sequential **upward motion** and motivic development matter more than periodic song form. Endings can stay **modal or inconclusive**.

**Harmonic colour:** **Late-romantic action harmony**: dominants with alterations, ninths, augmented colour, **chromatic mediants**, Mixolydian touches, and jazz-tinted stacks—not pure diatonic Americana.

**Register:** **Dramatically wide**—the quartet can cover a large distance from low cello to high violin in a single phrase. This is one of the **broader** film profiles before you reach Herrmann.

**Best paired with:** **Schneider** (primary) or **Brookmeyer** (secondary)—orchestral scale and linear development; Schneider especially when you want full cinematic colour.

**Sounds like:** *Planet of the Apes*, *Chinatown*, *Star Trek: The Motion Picture*, *Total Recall*.

**Not this if you want:** Minimalist repetition, intimate narrow-register lounge writing, or strict four-bar classical periodicity.

## Béla Bartók

**In one sentence:** The native **modern string-quartet** voice—four truly independent parts, folk-angular melody, chromatic bass motion, and contrapuntal strictness rather than a Hollywood film-stub recipe.

**Phrase shape:** **Suite-like sections** with **strict imitation** and irregular, folk-influenced rhythm. Lines interlock; phrases behave like chamber-modernist discourse, not like a film theme with a single tune and accompaniment. Cadence logic follows **Bartók quartet grammar**, not film-loop or blockbuster grammar.

**Harmonic colour:** **Chromatic, axis- and folk-inflected** harmony—dissonance is controlled but present; parallel fifths are treated more strictly than in most film stubs. Not a “underscore mood” palette; closer to **concert quartet** language.

**Register:** **Wide and agile**—cello and violin can occupy strongly contrasting registers with angular leaps. Spread is **wide**, similar in ambition to Goldsmith but with **modernist** rather than Hollywood jazz harmony.

**Best paired with:** **Brookmeyer** or **Mingus** when translating to big band—linear/contrapuntal BB styles that can respect independent voices. **Schneider** works for colour but may smooth some of Bartók's bite; **Basie** is usually a poor fit for this source material.

**Sounds like:** Bartók's string quartets (especially the folk-modern middle period), not a specific film franchise.

**Not this if you want:** A ready-made film underscore cliché, pedal-drenched minimalism, or a single soprano tune with static inner parts.

## John Williams

**In one sentence:** Heroic, leitmotif-driven melody with periodic phrasing, strong cadences, and harmony that supports adventure-film grandeur.

**Phrase shape:** **Romantic periodic phrases**—clear **eight-bar periodicity** is welcome, and **authentic cadences** read as success, not as old-fashioned mistakes. Themes **develop** like leitmotifs: recognizable cells return at climaxes.

**Harmonic colour:** **Tonal adventure harmony**: dominants, secondary dominants, **chromatic mediants**, Lydian and Neapolitan colour, quartal fanfare stacks, pandiatonic brightness—functional backbone with cinematic extensions.

**Register:** **Heroic arch** in the top line—including **fanfare leaps** at peaks. Accompaniment supports a soaring melody; total spread is **wide** but more “orchestral hero” than Goldsmith's volatile chromaticism.

**Best paired with:** **Schneider** (primary) or **Brookmeyer** (secondary)—full orchestral BB translation; Schneider when you want maximum cinematic breadth.

**Sounds like:** *Star Wars*, *Schindler's List*, *E.T.*, *Jurassic Park*.

**Not this if you want:** Ambiguous modal loops, whisper-narrow intimacy, or thriller cluster ostinatos.

## Hans Zimmer

**In one sentence:** Cathartic **ostinato** texture—repeating cells that accumulate density and register height over time, in a minor/modal world with almost no classical cadence.

**Phrase shape:** **Repeating ostinato cells** (roughly four to eight bars) that **cycle many times**; new entries stack on each repetition so the texture **thickens** without changing the harmonic recipe every bar. **No strong perfect cadence**—aeolian cadence flavour (iv–v–i colour) is as “closed” as it gets. Continuous sound is correct; rests are not the point.

**Harmonic colour:** **Aeolian / natural minor** cycling (Am–Em–G–D type motion), **♭VI submediant colour**, chromatic mediants, triads with add-tone sheen. Inner voices may sit on **static pads** while the top line and bass ostinato move—a **cathartic stack**, not traditional four-part counterpoint.

**Register:** A **widening arc**: starts contained, grows **accumulatively** louder and higher across repetitions—can reach **wide** spread at peak (especially in dark-action cues). Parallel octaves in the ascent (Shepard-tone flavour) are allowed.

**Best paired with:** **Schneider** (primary) or **Brookmeyer** (secondary)—large-scale orchestration that can carry massed repetition; avoid arranging styles that demand short, cadential jazz heads.

**Sounds like:** *Interstellar*, *Inception*, *The Dark Knight*, *Gladiator*.

**Not this if you want:** Neat four-bar song form, cool-jazz pedal intimacy (Badalamenti), or twelve-tone independence (Bartók).

*Note:* A **passacaglia-style** variant (ground bass with voices entering in layers) is documented in the profile for advanced use; behaviour is denser and slightly wider in register when that mode applies.

## Bernard Herrmann

**In one sentence:** Obsessive **short cells**, extreme registers, and biting dissonance—thriller and fantasy underscore where the quartet feels like a single hypnotic machine.

**Phrase shape:** **Very short ostinato cells** (often one to two bars) that repeat and grind; closure can be **dissonant or weak** rather than tonal. The profile rewards **motif recurrence** and hypnotic repetition over lyrical development.

**Harmonic colour:** **Polytriads**, **tritone anchors**, high **clusters**, and chromatic shifts—Hitchcock-era thriller harmony, not mainstream tonality. Dissonance is a feature, not something to smooth away.

**Register:** **Extreme**—low and high registers are used deliberately; the quartet's total height is the **widest** among common Harmonic Voice choices.

**Best paired with:** **Brookmeyer** (primary) or **Mingus** (secondary)—Brookmeyer for linear unease; Mingus for angular, heavy expression. **Schneider** can work for colouristic thriller orchestration; **Basie** is usually a mismatch for this source.

**Sounds like:** *Psycho*, *Vertigo*, *The Day the Earth Stood Still*, *The Ghost and Mrs. Muir*.

**Not this if you want:** Warm folk melody, gentle modal pedals, or heroic cadential themes.

## How to choose

- **Narrow, repeating, meditative:** Glass.  
- **Singable tune, clear phrases:** Rota or Williams (Williams is bigger and more cadential).  
- **Chamber sophistication:** Ravel.  
- **Dreamy pedal + jazz haze:** Badalamenti.  
- **Wide-screen modal melody:** Morricone.  
- **Volatile chromatic action:** Goldsmith.  
- **Concert modernism:** Bartók.  
- **Ostinato mass and climb:** Zimmer.  
- **Thriller hypnosis:** Herrmann.

Set **SQ Harmonic Voice** before you sketch; set **BB Arranging Style** before you translate. If the combination feels wrong, trust the compatibility hints in the SQ→BB panel and try Schneider for most film stubs, Brookmeyer for Herrmann (and often Bartók), and avoid Basie when the source material is modernist or non-swing.
