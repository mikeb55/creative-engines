# COMPOSER OS V3.3 — ADVANCED COMPOSITION RESEARCH AND IMPLEMENTATION SPECIFICATION

Prepared 16 October 2026 · Repository audited: [`mikeb55/creative-engines`](https://github.com/mikeb55/creative-engines) at `main` = `8a50369b` · Baseline smoke: 797 pass rows, 143 suites, 5 known fails, 0 unexpected ([V3.2 Final Release Report](https://github.com/mikeb55/creative-engines/blob/8a50369b/docs/V3.2-FINAL-RELEASE-REPORT.md))

## 1. Verdict and scope

**Ready for implementation, with three conditions.**

1. Build in the order given in section 9. Each stage ends with a green smoke run.
2. No existing generator's output may change. Every new module is **analysis-first or opt-in**, and existing golden fingerprints must stay byte-identical with the new flags off.
3. Nothing in V3.3 grants artistic approval. Every new output carries `musicallyApproved: false` until Mike records a human review.

### What the audit found

The repository already contains most of the musical machinery V3.3 needs, spread across separate engines. It holds 1,666 files under `engines/composer-os-v2/core` alone, and the largest groups are:

| Group | Files |
|---|---|
| song-mode | 146 |

| pentatonic | 59 |

The real gaps are not more generators. Four things are missing:

- **One shared motivic vocabulary.** Transformations are implemented four separate times, in motif, BHL, pentatonic and the Berg row engine. Nothing guarantees the four agree.
- **A guitar-aware chord-melody path outside Barry Harris.** Playability checks exist only in `barry-harris/guitarVoicingFeasibility.ts`.
- **Counterpoint evidence for any two or more voices.** Today's checks are tied to SATB generation, or limited to guitar–bass in BHL evidence.
- **A coherence view across a whole piece** that reads motif, harmony, texture and form together without inventing a quality score.

V3.3 therefore unifies, extends and exposes what is already there. It builds one new composition surface on top of existing engines and does not create a new theory engine.

### Out of scope

- V3.2 manuals, Practice Studio and the scale glossary: finished.
- Desktop signing and packaging.
- Any change to locked WWH, BHL source or Performance Composer scores.

## 3. Research basis (what V3.3 encodes, and why)

### 3.1 Motivic development

- **Schoenberg's developing variation and *Grundgestalt*** are among the most far-reaching ideas in compositional theory. A piece grows from a basic shape through continuous, cumulative variation rather than literal repetition ([Almada, *Per Musi*](https://www.scielo.br/j/pm/a/KjBrYfZxNKMQQtgWYTWFcQF/?lang=en&ilang=en); [Frisch, Columbia Academic Commons](https://academiccommons.columbia.edu/doi/10.7916/D87P9907/download)).
- **What this means for V3.3:**
  - A transformation is a step in a *chain*, and each new form derives from the previous form, not always from the original.
  - The chain must be recorded so a score can show how bar 29 descends from bar 1.
  - Coherence is a lineage you can read, not a similarity score.
- **Pattern discovery.** The SIA/SIATEC/COSIATEC family finds *translatable patterns* (repeated shapes under transposition and time shift) in a point-set view of the score. It was evaluated on the JKU Patterns Development Database ([Meredith, MIREX 2016](http://www.titanmusic.com/papers/public/MeredithMIREX2016.pdf); [Meredith, Aalborg](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/181893482/DM10.pdf)). V3.3 uses a bounded, deterministic version of this idea for motif *discovery* in imported scores, alongside the existing exact-cell matchers.

### 3.2 Counterpoint and independence

- **Huron's perceptual principles** derive the classical voice-leading rules from how listeners hear streams ([Huron, *Tone and Voice*](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/40a2/47c5e711f4296e09549cf2306730621e5588.pdf)):
  - pitch proximity, normally 2 semitones or less for streaming;
  - tonal fusion, meaning avoid unisons, octaves and perfect fifths for independence;
  - onset synchrony, meaning avoid onsets closer than 100 ms;
  - limited density, three parts or fewer for easy tracking;
  - pitch co-modulation, meaning parallel motion fuses voices.
- **Fux's species rules** (no parallel perfect consonances, prefer contrary and oblique motion, prepare and resolve dissonance) are the historical form of the same constraints ([Fux exercises summary](https://docslib.org/doc/7408711/species-counterpoint-exercises-from-fuxs-gradus-ad-parnassum)).
- **What this means for V3.3:**
  - An independence report counts *perceptual* fusion risks (co-onsets, parallel perfect intervals, co-modulation runs, crossings, density over three voices) per voice pair and per bar.
  - It reports **evidence, not prohibitions**. BHL already treats dissonance as context, and V3.3 does the same for parallels, since parallel motion is often a deliberate colour (Brookmeyer allows fifths per `INVARIANTS.md`).

### 3.3 Hooks and memorability

- **Catchiness** is defined as "long-term musical salience, the degree to which a musical fragment remains memorable after a period of time" ([Burgoyne et al., *Hooked*](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/items/498e979f-42b4-418d-9a30-eb01ac64931f), quoted via [APA](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/aca-aca0000090.pdf)).
- **Expectation models** such as IDyOM quantify how predictable each note is from learned style statistics ([Pearce & Wiggins 2012](https://www.marcus-pearce.com/assets/papers/PearceWiggins2012.pdf)).
- **What this means for V3.3:**
  - Hook evidence reports observable correlates: the number of returns, the position of the first statement, rhythmic and contour identity across returns, register peak, and contrast with surrounding material.
  - It **must not** claim to predict catchiness.
  - An IDyOM-style expectation model is **DEFER**. It needs a trained corpus and validation that do not exist here.

### 3.4 Chord melody on guitar

- The existing feasibility rule (4-fret span, 5-fret stretch, one note per string) is a sound baseline for physical feasibility.
- **Radical chord melody** (unrelated triads, triad pairs, two-note seventh shapes, open-string colour) needs three things:
  - the melody on top, with voicings generated *under* it;
  - per-tuning fret solving;
  - an explicit "stretch" or "impossible" verdict, rather than silent octave displacement.
- **Worked check.** C major triad, top note E4 (MIDI 64), on strings B/G/D at fret 5: B5 = E4, G5 = C4, D5 = G3. D major triad, top note F♯4: B7 = F♯4, G7 = D4, D7 = A3. Both are a 1-fret span, and the move between them is a parallel shift of two frets. This is the first triad-pair fixture.

## 5. Implementation specification

### 5.1 N1 Motivic Operator Library

```ts
// core/motif-ops/types.ts
export interface MotifNoteEvent { onset: number; duration: number; midi: number | null; } // quarters; null = rest
export interface Motif { id: string; events: readonly MotifNoteEvent[]; field?: readonly number[]; } // field = pitch-class collection for in-field ops
export type OperatorId =
  | 'transpose' | 'transpose-in-field' | 'invert' | 'invert-in-field' | 'retrograde' | 'retrograde-inversion'
  | 'augment' | 'diminish' | 'fragment-head' | 'fragment-tail' | 'fragment-inner'
  | 'sequence-real' | 'sequence-tonal' | 'expand-intervals' | 'contract-intervals'
  | 'displace' | 'insert-rest' | 'register-lift' | 'liquidate';
export interface TransformStep { op: OperatorId; params: Record<string, number | string>; fromId: string; toId: string; }
export interface OperatorResult { motif: Motif; step: TransformStep; warnings: string[]; } // e.g. "pitch left field", "rest at onset 0"
export function applyOperator(m: Motif, op: OperatorId, params?: Record<string, number | string>): OperatorResult;
```

**Rules:**

- Every operator is pure, with no randomness. Rests are preserved. Out-of-field pitches are named in `warnings` and never dropped silently.
- In-field operations map by scale degree within `field`. Chromatic operations map by semitone.
- **Inversion axis:** the first sounding pitch by default; `params.axis` (MIDI) overrides it.
- **Sequence:** `steps` and `count`. A tonal sequence uses in-field steps. Each repetition joins end to end.
- **Expand/contract:** adds or subtracts `amount` to each interval's magnitude, keeping direction. In-field mode counts degrees.
- **Liquidate:** progressively drops the last n events over `count` statements (Schoenberg's liquidation) and returns the chain.

### 5.2 N2 Development chains

```ts
export interface DevelopmentChain { rootId: string; steps: TransformStep[]; results: Motif[]; } // results[i] = after steps[0..i]
export function buildChain(root: Motif, plan: Array<{ op: OperatorId; params?: Record<string, number | string> }>): DevelopmentChain;
```

Lineage IDs are deterministic (`m1`, then `m1.r`, then `m1.r.fh`). When a chain is placed in a score, each placed statement carries `{lineageId, bar, beat}` in the receipt.

### 5.3 N3 Motif discovery

- **Input:** a `CanonicalEvaluationScore` and a part filter.
- **Representation:** points (onset in quarters, MIDI), one set per part.
- **Algorithm:** for windows of up to 64 events, compute difference vectors and group translatable patterns (SIA-style), keeping patterns of 3–12 notes with at least 2 occurrences. Rank by `coverage = occurrences × length`.
- **Limits:** cap at 20 patterns per part. Order deterministically by (coverage desc, first onset asc, pitch vector lexicographic).
- **Output:** `{ patterns: [{ id, points, occurrences: [{bar, beat, transposition}] }], limits: {windowCap: 64, truncated: boolean} }`.
- **Performance:** a 200-bar quartet must finish in under 2 s on the dev machine. The test enforces about 4,000 events.

### 5.4 N4 Radical chord-melody voicer

```ts
export type VoicingSource = 'barry-harris' | 'triad-pair' | 'unrelated-triads' | 'shell-dyads' | 'upper-structure';
export interface ChordMelodyRequest {
  melody: readonly MotifNoteEvent[];             // top voice, sounding pitch
  harmony: ReadonlyArray<{ onset: number; symbol?: string; collectionMask?: number; triads?: string[] }>;
  source: VoicingSource; tuningId: string;      // existing tuning ids
  maxVoices: 2 | 3 | 4; maxSpan?: number;       // default 4, stretch allowed to 5 with flag
  voiceEvery: 'each-note' | 'strong-beats' | 'harmony-changes';
}
export interface GripChoice { onset: number; midis: number[]; frets: Array<{ string: number; fret: number }>; span: number; stretch: boolean; }
export interface ChordMelodyResult { musicXml: string; grips: GripChoice[]; unvoiced: Array<{ onset: number; reason: string }>; receipt: object; }
```

**Rules:**

- **The melody is always the top sounding note**, and it is never altered.
- When `source = 'barry-harris'`, delegate to `runBarryHarrisChordMelodyPipeline`.
- For triad-pair, select the triad of the pair that contains the melody pitch class. If both do, prefer the one that differs from the previous grip.
- Unrelated triads come from BHL world F or a user list.
- **Voice leading:** choose the candidate that minimises the sum of absolute semitone movement of inner voices (reuse `scoreTymoczkoHorizontal` from `counterpoint/voiceLeadingScorer.ts`). Break ties by lower span, then lower fret.
- **Impossible grips** are left as a single melody note and recorded in `unvoiced`, never moved silently.

### 5.5 N5 Independence report and X3 voice relations

```ts
export interface PairIndependence {
  pair: [string, string];
  motion: { contrary: number; oblique: number; similar: number; parallel: number };   // proportions
  coOnsetRate: number;                // shared attacks / union of attacks
  parallelPerfectRuns: Array<{ startBar: number; length: number; interval: 'P1' | 'P5' | 'P8' }>;
  coModulationRuns: Array<{ startBar: number; length: number }>;                      // ≥3 consecutive same-interval moves
  crossings: number; overlaps: number;
  attackJaccard: number;              // rhythmic independence
}
export interface IndependenceReport { version: 'independence-1'; parts: string[]; pairs: PairIndependence[]; densityOver3Bars: number[]; notes: string[]; }
```

The report uses no thresholds and no pass/fail. The dashboard shows the numbers with a plain-English note, for example "Violin I and Viola move in parallel fifths for 3 attacks from bar 12. This often fuses the two lines."

### 5.6 X6 Hook evidence and N6 coherence report

```ts
export interface HookEvidence { hookId: string; firstBar: number; returns: Array<{ bar: number; identity: 'exact' | 'transposed' | 'varied'; similarity: number }>;
  registerPeakBar: number | null; contrastBeforeReturn: number /* harmonic+rhythmic change, 0–1 */; }
export interface CoherenceReport { version: 'coherence-1'; lineage: Array<{ lineageId: string; bar: number; beat: number; op?: string }>;
  discovered: string[]; hooks: HookEvidence[]; sectionContrast: Array<{ from: string; to: string; harmonic: number; rhythmic: number }>;
  independence: IndependenceReport | null; absences: Array<{ cellId: string; fromBar: number; toBar: number }>; musicalQualityScore: null; }
```

### 5.7 N7 Development Studio (`#/develop`)

The page is laid out top to bottom. Its terms match the V3.2 Practice Studio (Play, Make another, Variation).

1. **Load.** From the latest Generate output, a BHL candidate, a Hexatonic or Pentatonic export, or Open MusicXML.
2. **Cell.** Select bars and a part, or pick from "Found in this score" (N3).
3. **Develop.** Operator buttons in plain names (Turn upside down, Backwards, Stretch, Squeeze, Take the start, Take the end, Step it up, Widen, Narrow, Shift the beat, Add a breath, Lift, Wind down). Each press adds a chain step, shown as a horizontal lineage strip with ▶ Play on each step.
4. **Harmonise.** Opens N4 with source choices (Barry Harris, Triad pair, Unrelated triads, Shells, Upper structures) and a tuning. Shows the grip strip and any unvoiced notes.
5. **Send.** Practice this, Open in BHL (MusicXML), Evaluate (opens the Coherence tab), Export MusicXML.

Status badges are always visible: "Not musically approved" until a human review exists.

## 7. Evaluation contract

1. Every new report has `musicalQualityScore: null` and `musicallyApproved: false`.
2. Reports describe, and only humans decide. BMEF decisions stay in `humanReviewRecord.ts`.
3. Each report records its version, input SHA-256 and parameters, so it is reproducible (same as existing receipts).
4. Plain-English notes use neutral verbs ("moves", "returns", "fuses", "is absent"). Value words ("good", "weak", "better") are banned. A unit test checks for them.
5. GCE, LOCK and DCR remain the only gates on generators. V3.3 adds no new LOCK gate.

## 8. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Golden fingerprint drift | Flags off by default; F15 test; adapters do not touch generator code paths |
| Duplicate transform semantics diverge | Adapter tests assert that BHL and pentatonic transforms equal N1 results on shared fixtures, or document the deliberate difference |
| Discovery is slow on big scores | Window cap, event cap and a timing test |
| Chord-melody voicings unplayable | X2 solver with a span cap and visible `unvoiced` notes; listening and playing tests by Mike are separate |
| Evidence read as judgement | Wording rule, null score, approval badge |
| UTF-16 file breaks typecheck | X7 first |
