# How the panel works now

As of this build, the Style Pairings panel is single\-select, not stackable:

- Only one voice can be active at a time\.
- Default on first load is None — no voice is applied unless you pick one\.
- Selecting a new voice automatically deselects whichever one was active before\.
- This panel is independent of the normal VRB dropdown — switching VRBs elsewhere does not touch your style pairing selection, and vice versa\.

If you want the app's plain, unstyled generation behavior, leave the panel on None\.

# 10 scenarios showing the range

### 1\. Blues shuffle, static I\-IV\-V vamp

__Voice: Hendrix\. __Call\-and\-response fills and blues ornamentation work best over a repeating chord vamp where the “response” has room to land between chord changes\.

### 2\. Dense riff\-driven blues section

__Voice: Led Zeppelin\. __Same harmonic family as Hendrix but heavier and more riff\-based — use this when the underlying track already has weight and you want the lead to match it rather than answer it\.

### 3\. Odd\-meter jazz\-fusion passage

__Voice: Mahavishnu\. __Built for angular, high\-energy fusion lines — this is the voice to reach for when the meter isn’t 4/4 and you want technical density\.

### 4\. Funk pocket, rhythm\-forward part

__Voice: Nile Rodgers\. __Tight, groove\-locked phrasing designed for sitting in the pocket rather than soloing over it — good for rhythm guitar parts in funk or soul tracks\.

### 5\. Heavier funk\-rock riff

__Voice: Iommi\. __Where Nile Rodgers is pure pocket, Iommi adds rock weight — use this when the funk needs an edge, not just a groove\.

### 6\. Prog build with tension

__Voice: Fripp\. __Diminished\-scale lines create instability — reach for this in a build section where you want unresolved tension before a release\.

### 7\. Slow, expressive solo

__Voice: Gilmour\. __Melodic and sustain\-heavy rather than dense — best for a solo that needs room to breathe, not one packed with notes\.

### 8\. Jazz standard or walking blues

__Voice: Bill Evans sax\. __Bebop vocabulary over a jazz\-blues foundation — use this on more harmonically sophisticated changes than the rock\-oriented voices are built for\.

### 9\. Technical showcase solo

__Voice: EVH\. __Built for speed and virtuosic runs — this is the voice for a section that’s meant to impress technically, not sit back in a groove\.

### 10\. Southern rock jam outro

__Voice: Jimmy Herring\. __Southern rock with a jam\-band feel — best on extended outro sections where the solo has space to develop over time\. Also has an optional Dickinson flavor post\-process layer for extra call\-and\-response and triplet pickups — off by default, opt\-in only\.

# Quick tips

- Switching voices mid\-session doesn't require a restart — the panel updates live\.
- If a voice sounds off for a section, try its nearest genre neighbor from the table above before troubleshooting — most “wrong” results are really just a style mismatch, not a bug\.
- The panel governs style pairing only\. VRB selection in the main dropdown is a separate, independent choice\.
